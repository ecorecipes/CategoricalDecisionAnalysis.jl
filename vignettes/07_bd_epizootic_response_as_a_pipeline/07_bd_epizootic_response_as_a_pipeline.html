Motivation

The last vignette of DecisionAnalysis.jl (its number 14) analyses the response to an outbreak of Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis (Bd), the fungus that causes chytridiomycosis, in a frog pond, from a cached table of model runs. This vignette takes the same table, writes the analysis as a pipeline, checks that the pipeline gives the same numbers, and then does three things that are awkward without the diagram: a survey as a chain of observation processes, a limit on how many frogs the field team can handle, and a statement of what an error in the weights can cost.

What this is and is not

  • The model is the stochastic individual-based model of Drawert, Griesemer, Petzold and Briggs (2017, J. R. Soc. Interface 14:20170480, doi:10.1098/rsif.2017.0480), as implemented in Chytridiomycosis.jl. The parameter points are the authors’.
  • The runs are not the authors’: Chytridiomycosis.jl ran the model at 100 of the authors’ 2,132 parameter points under ten actions, 30 times each, and stores the result. Numbers read from that table are labelled from the cached table.
  • Everything else is illustrative: the weights on the groups, the utility, every number that describes the survey, and the handling limit. Nothing here is a published result, and none of it is advice for a real pond.

The currency is frogs. No cost in money appears: none is published for this system.

The cached table, by group

The actions are no intervention (none, the baseline) and one pulse at 75% coverage of one of three kinds (treat the frogs with an antifungal, cull frogs, clean the water) on day 14, 28 or 56 after Bd arrives. The authors sort their parameter points into five groups, A to E, by what the model does there without intervention (A: the frogs always go extinct; D: Bd usually dies out; B, C and E in between or variable).

The core analysis, in DecisionAnalysis alone: the utility is the number of frogs alive at the end of a run, and conditional_problem collapses the draws onto the group.

What the pipeline adds

1. A survey as a chain: swabs, assay, report

A survey before the decision does not reveal the group. It goes through three steps, each of which loses something. Every number in this section is illustrative.

  1. Swabs. Frogs are swabbed; the infection load on the swabs (none, low, high) depends on the group.
  2. Assay. A qPCR classifies each batch of swabs, with errors.
  3. Report. The laboratory reports positive or negative, and calls a low qPCR signal positive only half the time.
load = :swab_load => [:none, :low, :high]
qpcr = :qpcr => [:negative, :low_positive, :high_positive]
report = :report => [:negative, :positive]
#                                    A     B     C     D     E
swabs = channel_box(:swabs, group, load, [0.05 0.10 0.20 0.60 0.30
                                          0.25 0.40 0.50 0.30 0.40
                                          0.70 0.50 0.30 0.10 0.30])
assay = channel_box(:assay, load, qpcr, [0.95 0.20 0.02; 0.05 0.70 0.18; 0.00 0.10 0.80])
reporting = channel_box(:reporting, qpcr, report, [1.0 0.5 0.0; 0.0 0.5 1.0])
surveyed = decision_pipeline(pond, swabs, assay, reporting)

The value cannot rise along the chain: each stage is the one before passed through a noisy step. Here it is zero from the swabs onwards. No survey result changes the best action, because the decision hardly depends on the group, and even perfect knowledge of the group is worth a fraction of a frog. The diagram also says, without any arithmetic, that the report adds nothing once the qPCR result is read:

Suppose the field team can handle, on average over the ponds it is responsible for, at most 20 frogs per pond (illustrative). Early treatment handles about 70. That limit is a side condition on the policy, which the rule-based analysis has no place for; in the compiled form of the diagram it is one more constraint.

limit = expectation_constraint(:field_team, (action, group), handled.payoff.Q; at_most=20)
function under_limit(p)
    s = solve_policy_program(p; constraints=[limit])
    return (frogs=s.value, deterministic=s.deterministic, policy=s.policy)
end
unread = under_limit(pond)
rows = Any[["nothing", unread.frogs, 0.0, unread.frogs - from_pipeline.scores[1]]]
for w in stages
    r = under_limit(reading(w))
    push!(rows, [w, r.frogs, r.frogs - unread.frogs, r.frogs - from_pipeline.scores[1]])
end

With the limit the value is positive and falls at every step of the chain; by the time the result has been reduced to a positive or negative report almost none is left. The conclusion that a survey is worthless was a conclusion about a manager with no limit on handling.

3. What an error in the weights can cost

The design weights are an assumption. Suppose the true weights of the groups differ from them by at most ε in total variation (an ε share of the probability moved from some groups to others). The diagram returns a bound on the frogs lost by deciding with the design weights.

function bound_at(ε)
    stated = with_error(pond, :design_weights => ε)
    return (ε=ε, pipeline_bound=fidelity_bound(stated),
            sharper_bound=decision_loss_bound(by_group, ε))
end
[bound_at(ε) for ε in (0.01, 0.05, 0.1)]
3-element Vector{@NamedTuple{ε::Float64, pipeline_bound::Float64, sharper_bound::Float64}}:
 (ε = 0.01, pipeline_bound = 1.5694202898550722, sharper_bound = 1.5547212672733397)
 (ε = 0.05, pipeline_bound = 7.847101449275361, sharper_bound = 7.773606336366699)
 (ε = 0.1, pipeline_bound = 15.694202898550722, sharper_bound = 15.547212672733398)

The pipeline’s bound uses the range of the count over all actions and groups; the core’s decision_loss_bound uses the largest range of a single action and is sharper when the payoff table is at hand (here the two are close). Both are guarantees and both are loose: at ε = 0.01 the bound is about a frog and a half, and at ε = 0.05 it already exceeds the gain of the best action over no intervention. The loss that actually occurs for a few particular shifts of the weights:

weights = probabilities(by_group.belief)
function actual_loss(shifted)
    truth = replace_box(pond, :design_weights, shifted)
    chosen = first_choice(decide(pond, MaxExpectedUtility()))
    return act_now_value(truth) - pipeline_value(truth; policies=(:manager => chosen))
end
shift(from, to, ε) = (w = copy(weights); w[from] -= ε; w[to] += ε; w)
md_table(["shift of 0.1 of the weight", "frogs lost by deciding with the design weights"],
         [["from A to D", actual_loss(shift(1, 4, 0.1))], ["from A to C", actual_loss(shift(1, 3, 0.1))],
          ["from D to B", actual_loss(shift(4, 2, 0.1))]]; digits=4)

from A to D 0

For errors in the weights of more than a few per cent the bound cannot certify the choice, and the direct check is the informative one: the choice does not move under these shifts.

Summary

  • The pipeline reproduces the core analysis: the same expected survivors, the same best action, the same regret table and value of perfect information, the same robust choices.
  • A survey written as a chain of three boxes has a value that cannot rise from one step to the next, and the graph shows which readings are redundant.
  • A handling limit is one constraint in the compiled diagram. It changes the answer to “is a survey worth it”: without the limit the survey is worth nothing, with it the survey helps to decide which ponds are treated.
  • The fidelity bound is stated with its limits: here it certifies the choice only for small errors in the weights.
