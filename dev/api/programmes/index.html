CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.ExpectationConstraint — Type
ExpectationConstraint

A linear constraint on a compiled pipeline: a bound on the expectation, under the policy being chosen, of a quantity that is a function of some wires. Build one with expectation_constraint (a budget in individuals handled, a floor on an expected count) or chance_constraint (a cap on the probability of an event). Fields: name, wires, values (an array over the wires' labels, or a function of the labels), sense (:at_most or :at_least) and bound.

These are constraints that the influence-diagram solvers of the ecosystem (optimize, contextual_optimize) cannot express: they maximise expected utility over all policies, with no side condition.

PolicySolution

What solve_policy_program returns: status (:optimal, :infeasible, ...), value (the optimal expected utility), policy (the matrix π[a, z], rows = actions, columns = joint observations), actions and observations (the labels of its rows and columns), deterministic (whether every column of the policy is a single action), exact and route (as in DecisionAnalysis's ProgramSolution).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.StrategyProgram — Type
StrategyProgram

A pipeline, or a chain of pipelines in series, compiled to a mixed-integer linear programme over deterministic policies: what strategy_program returns. Fields:

  • parts: the pipelines of the chain, in order.
  • model: the JuMP.GenericModel{T}.
  • policies: for each open policy box, the array of binary variables z[a, observations...].
  • flows: for each part, the dictionary from joint wire values (a CartesianIndex) to the flow variable of that path.
  • output_mass: the expressions for the probability of each joint output of the last part (in joint_states order), the variables a further part would share.
  • objective: the objective expression, the sum of the parts' expected utilities.
  • constraints: the side constraints that were compiled.

compose of two strategy programmes is the programme of the chain: the output mass of the first is identified with the input mass of the second and the objectives are added.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.StrategySolution — Type
StrategySolution

What solve_strategy_program returns: status, value (the expected utility of the strategy, recomputed exactly from the pipeline), policies (for each decision box, the array of action labels, one per joint observation), route (:enumeration or :optimizer), exact (whether optimality is an exact statement: true for enumeration, false for an optimiser, whose strategy is feasible and whose value is exact but whose optimality rests on the optimiser) and strategies (the number examined by enumeration, or nothing).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.chance_constraint — Method
chance_constraint(name, wires, event; at_most=nothing, at_least=nothing)
    -> ExpectationConstraint

The constraint P(event) ≤ at_most or ≥ at_least for a compiled pipeline, where event is a predicate on the labels of wires: for example that the probability of local extinction stays below one in twenty. It is the expectation constraint of the event's indicator, and so linear in the policy.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.expectation_constraint — Method
expectation_constraint(name, wires, values; at_most=nothing, at_least=nothing)
    -> ExpectationConstraint

The constraint E[values(wires)] ≤ at_most or ≥ at_least (exactly one of the two) for a compiled pipeline. wires is a wire or a tuple of wires (names, or name => labels pairs) and values an array with one axis per wire or a function of the wires' labels. Typical uses: a budget counted in individuals or effort (the expected number of animals handled is at most the capacity of the field team) and a floor (the expected number of survivors is at least a target).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.policy_program — Method
policy_program(p::DecisionPipeline; constraints=[]) -> DecisionProgram

Compile a pipeline with one open policy box and no input wires to a linear programme, as a DecisionProgram of DecisionAnalysis (a JuMP.GenericModel{T}, T = Rational{BigInt} for a pipeline with integer or rational tables). The decision box becomes the variables, the chance boxes the coefficients, the utility boxes the objective:

maximise    Σ_{a,z} J[a, z] π[a, z]
subject to  Σ_a π[a, z] = 1          for every joint observation z,    (simplex[z])
            Σ_{a,z} G_c[a, z] π[a, z] ≤ b_c   (or ≥) for every constraint c,
            π[a, z] ≥ 0,

where π[a, z] is the probability of action a after observing z (a stochastic policy; z runs over the joint values of the wires the policy box reads), J[a, z] = E[utility · 1{Z = z} | do(a)], and G_c is the same for the quantity of the ExpectationConstraint c. variables.π is the matrix of variables (rows = actions, columns = joint observations in joint_states order), and constraints.simplex and constraints.side the constraint references.

Without side constraints the optimum is attained at a deterministic policy and equals value_with_information(p), the optimum that contextual_optimize of CategoricalInfluenceDiagrams finds by enumerating policies (tested on every small problem). With a side constraint the best policy may have to randomise, which is why the variables are probabilities.

Solve it with DecisionAnalysis's solve_program (the built-in exact simplex by default, or any JuMP optimiser), or read the policy back with solve_policy_program. Shape: n_a · n_z variables and n_z plus the number of side constraints constraints.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.solve_policy_program — Method
solve_policy_program(p::DecisionPipeline; constraints=[], optimizer=nothing, rounding_tol)
    -> PolicySolution

Compile the one-decision pipeline with policy_program and solve it with DecisionAnalysis's solve_program: by default the built-in exact simplex, otherwise the JuMP optimizer given (an exact one, or a floating-point one whose solution is rounded and verified exactly). The policy is returned with its labels.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.solve_strategy_program — Method
solve_strategy_program(program::StrategyProgram; optimizer=nothing, max_strategies=100_000)
    -> StrategySolution

Solve a strategy programme.

  • optimizer = nothing (the default): exact enumeration. Every deterministic strategy is evaluated on the pipeline in exact arithmetic, those violating a side constraint are discarded, and the best is returned. No solver is needed; it is meant for small problems (more than max_strategies strategies throw CompilationError).
  • A mixed-integer optimiser (anything JuMP.set_optimizer accepts, for example HiGHS.Optimizer): the model is solved in Float64, the binary variables are read back as a strategy, and that strategy is then evaluated on the pipeline in exact arithmetic and its side constraints checked. The value returned is therefore exact for the strategy returned; that no better strategy exists is the optimiser's claim (exact = false).

The programme must be closed (no free input mass).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.strategy_program — Method
strategy_program(p::DecisionPipeline; constraints=[], T=nothing) -> StrategyProgram

Compile a pipeline with no input wires and any number of open policy boxes to a mixed-integer linear programme over deterministic policies, in the path formulation that DecisionProgramming.jl uses for influence diagrams (Salo, Andelmin and Oliveira 2022). With s a joint value of every wire, w(s) the product of the chance tables along it, and U(s) the sum of the utility boxes:

maximise    Σ_s U(s) φ(s)
subject to  Σ_a z_k[a, i] = 1                 for every decision k and observation i,
            φ(s) ≤ w(s),   φ(s) ≤ z_k[a_k(s), i_k(s)]   for every decision k,
            Σ_s φ(s) = 1,
            side constraints  Σ_s G_c(s) φ(s) ≤ b_c  (or ≥),
            z binary,  φ ≥ 0.

Decision boxes become the binary variables z (one local policy each), chance boxes the weights w, utility boxes the objective. For binary z the constraints force φ(s) = w(s) on the paths that the policies allow and zero on the others, so the feasible points are in bijection with the deterministic strategies and the objective is the expected utility. Decisions need not remember what earlier decisions saw or did (no perfect recall is assumed), which is why the programme is mixed-integer and not linear.

T is the number type of the model (Rational{BigInt} for integer or rational tables, Float64 otherwise, unless given). solve_strategy_program solves it.

Programmes compose. For pipelines in series, compose(strategy_program(p), strategy_program(q)) is one model in which the probability of each joint value of the shared wires is a shared quantity (the output mass of p's flow is the input mass of q's) and the objective is the sum of the two; this is the open-optimisation-problem construction of AlgebraicOptimization.jl (Hanks et al. 2024) specialised to decision diagrams. It has the same optimum as the programme of the glued pipeline strategy_program(compose(p, q)). Its flow variables are a sum over the parts where the glued programme has a product over all wires, which is smaller when the parts have many internal wires and a narrow interface, and can be larger when the interface carries most of the wires. What composes here is the programme, not its solution: the composite is solved as one model.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.fidelity_bound — Method
fidelity_bound(p::DecisionPipeline)

The decision-loss bound read off the diagram: 2 · B · Σ ε, with B the utility_range and Σ ε the total_error, computed by DecisionAnalysis's decision_loss_bound.

Meaning. Let the pipeline p be an approximation of a true pipeline of the same shape, in which each box of p is within its stated error of the true box (in kernel_distance, total variation). Then the policy that is best for p is worth, in the true pipeline, at most fidelity_bound(p) less than the policy that is best for the true pipeline. The errors add because total variation is sub-additive along composition and tensor, so the joint law of the diagram under any fixed policy moves by at most Σ ε; an expected utility then moves by at most B Σ ε, and comparing two policies costs the factor two. The bound is usually far from tight; the vignette on emulators shows the actual loss beside it.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.jensen_shannon_distance — Method
jensen_shannon_distance(p::AbstractVector, q::AbstractVector)

The Jensen-Shannon distance between two probability vectors: the square root of the Jensen-Shannon divergence ½ KL(p ‖ m) + ½ KL(q ‖ m), m = (p + q) / 2, in nats. It is a metric, lies in [0, √log 2], and bounds the total variation: total_variation(p, q) ≤ √2 · jensen_shannon_distance(p, q) (Pinsker's inequality applied to each half). Always a floating-point number.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.kernel_distance — Method
kernel_distance(K, K′; divergence=:total_variation)

The distance between two stochastic kernels with the same inputs and outputs: the largest distance, over the inputs, between the two output distributions,

D(K, K′) = max_x d(K[·, x], K′[·, x]).

K and K′ are column-stochastic matrices K[y, x] = P(y | x), arrays with the output axis first (the tables of pipeline boxes), FiniteKernels, or Kleisli maps of the distribution monad. With divergence = :total_variation (the default), d is DecisionAnalysis's total_variation, half the ℓ¹ distance, exact for rational kernels; with :jensen_shannon it is the jensen_shannon_distance.

Both are divergences that compose: sub-additive under sequential composition and under tensor,

D(f ; g, f′ ; g′) ≤ D(f, f′) + D(g, g′),      D(f ⊗ g, f′ ⊗ g′) ≤ D(f, f′) + D(g, g′)

(theorem T11 of the Lean development; after Perrone). This is the per-box error that fidelity_bound adds up.

The Kullback-Leibler divergence is not offered. In this supremum form it is also sub-additive along composition (by its chain rule; the test suite checks this on random kernels), so the reason is not that it fails to compose. It is that it is not a metric (not symmetric, no triangle inequality, infinite when supports differ) and bounds total variation only through a square root (Pinsker), so a sum of Kullback-Leibler errors does not give the linear loss bound 2 B Σ ε.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.total_error — Method
total_error(p::DecisionPipeline)

The sum of the stated errors (with_error) of the boxes that the expected utility depends on: every box upstream of a utility box (box_provenance). Errors of boxes that feed no utility are left out, because replacing such a box changes no expected utility.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.utility_range — Method
utility_range(p::DecisionPipeline)

The range B of the total utility of a pipeline: the largest minus the smallest value of the sum of its utility boxes over every joint value of the wires they read (whether or not the model gives it positive probability). It is the constant of fidelity_bound.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.export_fidelity_chain_certificate — Method
export_fidelity_chain_certificate(io_or_path, exact, approx; range, errors=nothing)

Write a "fidelity-chain-certificate/1" document: a chain of boxes on one finite state space, each given as its exact kernel and the approximation that replaces it (exact and approx are vectors of square column-stochastic matrices K[y, x] = P(y | x), first box first), a stated error for each box, the range B of the payoffs and the loss bound 2 · B · Σ errors.

The Lean checker accepts it exactly when every matrix is square of the same size and column-stochastic, every stated error is at least the total-variation distance between corresponding columns, the range is nonnegative and the bound is as stated. Then (theorem Certificate.RawFidelity.accepts_sound) for every payoff table on those states whose rows have range at most B and every prior on the input of the first box, an action that is best for the belief pushed through the approximate chain is within the bound of the best for the belief pushed through the exact chain.

errors defaults to the exact distances (kernel_distance); larger stated errors are allowed, smaller ones throw WitnessError. The matrices must be normalised: a truncated emulator whose columns do not sum to one is refused, because the additive bound is not the one proved for it. Entries that are not integers or rationals throw InexactCertificateError. The schema covers chains on one state space only; the bound that fidelity_bound reads off a general pipeline has no certificate yet.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.export_policy_certificate — Method
export_policy_certificate(io_or_path, problem::DecisionProblem, experiment::Experiment; policy=nothing)
export_policy_certificate(io_or_path, pipeline::DecisionPipeline; policy=nothing)

Write a "policy-certificate/1" document: a one-decision problem (payoff table of the feasible actions, prior, likelihood), an optimal policy policy[a, z] = P(a | z), a dual vector with one entry per signal, and the optimal value. The Lean checker (lake exe check_certificate, theorem Certificate.RawPolicy.accepts_sound) accepts it exactly when the policy is column-stochastic, the dual dominates every objective coefficient c[a, z] = Σ_θ p(θ) L[z, θ] Q[a, θ], and the policy's objective and the sum of the dual both equal the value; it then follows that the value is value_with_information(problem, experiment) and that no policy does better.

No solver is asked for duals: the dual is the column maxima of the coefficients (Lean policy_certificate_sound). policy defaults to the deterministic policy that takes the first best action after each signal; any optimal policy may be given instead (a matrix, rows = feasible actions, columns = signals), for example the one solve_policy_program found. For a pipeline the problem and the experiment are its decision_problem and observation_experiment.

The claim is verified in exact arithmetic before anything is written: a policy that is not column-stochastic or not optimal throws WitnessError, and entries that are not integers or rationals throw InexactCertificateError (both from DecisionAnalysis). Needs a single finite prior.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.finite_kernel — Function
finite_kernel(P::AbstractMatrix, input, output=input; orientation=:columns, atol) -> FiniteKernel
finite_kernel(m::ResilienceCore.MarkovKernel, input, output; atol) -> FiniteKernel

A stochastic matrix as a FiniteKernel from the wire input to the wire output (each written :name => labels, or a FiniteAxis).

  • With orientation = :columns (the default) the matrix is column-stochastic, P[y, x] = P(y | x): the convention of FiniteKernels and of the likelihood of an Experiment.
  • With orientation = :rows it is row-stochastic, P[x, y] = P(y | x): the convention of most Markov-chain and hidden-Markov-model code, and of ResilienceCore.MarkovKernel, whose method uses it.

The element type is kept, so a rational matrix gives an exact kernel. A matrix that is not stochastic in the stated orientation throws FiniteKernels' KernelNormalizationError. The table of the kernel (kernel.table) is what channel_box and consequence_box take.

A transition matrix maps a variable to itself at the next step, and the wires of a pipeline are named, so the two ends need different names (:state_t, :state_next) even when their labels are the same.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.BackwardInductionError — Type
BackwardInductionError(criterion, reason)

Thrown by backward_induction when solving stage by stage would not give a best policy for the criterion applied to the whole problem, and the caller did not ask for what it does give. criterion is its name and reason says why. For a criterion that is monotone but not an Eilenberg-Moore algebra (conditional value at risk, say) backward induction solves the nested rule, a different rule: pass nested = true to get that, labelled. For minimax regret pass heuristic = true to get the stage-by-stage answer, labelled as a heuristic. policy_search is exact for every criterion.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.CategoricalDecisionAnalysisError — Type
CategoricalDecisionAnalysisError

Abstract supertype of every exception that CategoricalDecisionAnalysis defines: MonadMismatchError, BackwardInductionError, PipelineStructureError, WireMismatchError, UnboundBoxError and CompilationError. It marks the package that introduced an error, not a kind of failure. Invalid arguments and keywords raise Base's ArgumentError instead, and the errors of DecisionAnalysis (under DecisionAnalysisError) and of FiniteKernels pass through unchanged.

CompilationError(msg)

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.MonadMismatchError — Type
MonadMismatchError(operation, expected, got, msg)

Thrown when two pieces that must live in the same uncertainty monad do not, and no monad morphism connects them: composing Kleisli maps of two monads, applying a structure map to a value of another monad, or pairing a belief with an aggregator that cannot read it (a credal set with the expectation algebra, say: there is no monad morphism from the credal monad to the distribution monad). Fields: operation (a Symbol), expected and got (the monads) and msg.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.PipelineStructureError — Type
PipelineStructureError(msg)

Thrown when a DecisionPipeline is not well formed: a wire produced by two boxes, a box reading a wire that does not exist, a cycle, a utility box with an output wire, or an interpreter asked for something the diagram's shape does not provide (a payoff table from a pipeline with two policy boxes, say).
