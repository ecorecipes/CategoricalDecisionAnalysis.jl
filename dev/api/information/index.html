CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.RegretCoarsening — Type
RegretCoarsening

What regret_coarsening returns. Fields (rows are feasible actions, columns the cells of the partition, everything weighted by the prior mass of the cell):

  • actions, cells: the labels of rows and columns.
  • mass: the prior probability of each cell.
  • coarse_payoff: Σ_{θ ∈ c} p(θ) Q[a, θ].
  • coarse_regret: the regret table of coarse_payoff, "regret after coarsening".
  • coarsened_regret: Σ_{θ ∈ c} p(θ) R[a, θ] with R the regret table of the problem, "coarsening after regret".
  • gap: one number per cell, coarsened_regret[a, c] − coarse_regret[a, c], the same for every action: the prior mass of the cell times the expected value of perfect information within the cell.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.coarsening_is_lossless — Method
coarsening_is_lossless(problem::DecisionProblem, partition::AbstractVector) -> Bool

Whether merging the states of each cell of partition loses nothing for this decision: every cell has an action that is best at every one of its states of positive probability. This is decided without computing any regret, and it is equivalent to the gap of regret_coarsening being zero in every cell, that is to evppi(problem, partition) == evpi(problem) (Lean coarseExpectedRegret_eq_expectedRegret_iff). Exact for rational problems; in floating point "best" is exact comparison, so prefer rational inputs.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.information_distance — Method
information_distance(e::Experiment, e′::Experiment; kw...)

Le Cam's distance between two experiments on the same states, max(deficiency(e, e′), deficiency(e′, e)). It is zero exactly when each is a garbling of the other. Each deficiency satisfies δ(e, e) = 0 and the triangle inequality δ(e, e″) ≤ δ(e, e′) + δ(e′, e″), which makes the experiments a Lawvere metric space, an enrichment of the garbling preorder (δ(e, e′) = 0 iff e′ ⪯ e). Keywords go to DecisionAnalysis's deficiency.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.information_value — Method
information_value(problem::DecisionProblem, experiment::Experiment)
information_value(problem::DecisionProblem, partition::AbstractVector)

The value of deciding after the information: the monotone map out of the garbling preorder (first form, DecisionAnalysis's value_with_information) and out of the refinement preorder (second form, the same for partition_experiment(partition)). Monotone means

is_garbling(e′, e)   ⟹  information_value(problem, e′) ≤ information_value(problem, e)
refines(fine, coarse) ⟹  information_value(problem, coarse) ≤ information_value(problem, fine)

for every problem with a single prior (Blackwell's theorem, easy direction: T6 and T4). EVPI, EVPPI and EVSI are this map at particular points, minus its value at the bottom of the preorder (DecisionAnalysis's act_now_value).

source
CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.is_garbling — Method
is_garbling(e′::Experiment, e::Experiment; kw...) -> Bool

Whether e′ ⪯ e in the garbling (Blackwell) preorder on experiments over the same states: e′ is e followed by a noisy channel, so e′ tells no more than e. The relation is reflexive and transitive (theorem T0 of the Lean development), and two experiments can each be a garbling of the other without being equal, so it is a preorder and not a partial order.

Decided by DecisionAnalysis's garbling_result (a linear programme; keywords such as optimizer are passed on), which also returns the witness matrix or, for an exact "no", a decision problem in which e′ is worth strictly more.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.partition_experiment — Method
partition_experiment(partition::AbstractVector; states=nothing) -> Experiment

The deterministic experiment that reveals the cell of a partition: one signal per cell, with likelihood one when the state is in the cell and zero otherwise. Learning the cell is this experiment, so the partial-information value of the partition is its sample-information value,

evppi(problem, partition) == evsi(problem, partition_experiment(partition)),

and a refinement of a partition is an experiment of which the coarser one is a garbling: the refinement preorder embeds in the garbling preorder.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.posterior_distribution — Method
posterior_distribution(prior, experiment::Experiment) -> FiniteDistribution

An experiment represented by the distribution it induces over posterior beliefs: a FiniteDistribution whose outcomes are posterior probability vectors over the states and whose probabilities are those of the signals that lead to them. Signals of probability zero do not appear, and signals with the same posterior are merged, so two experiments that differ only by relabelling or splitting signals have the same distribution. prior is a FiniteBelief or a probability vector.

The mean of the distribution is the prior (posterior_mean). The value of the experiment is the expectation, under this distribution, of the convex function "best expected payoff at the posterior" (value_at_posteriors). A garbling gives a distribution that is smaller in the convex order (a mean-preserving contraction), which is why one Jensen-type lemma gives EVPI ≥ 0, 0 ≤ EVSI ≤ EVPI and Blackwell's easy direction (after Fritz, Gonda, Perrone and Rischel).

source
CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.posterior_mean — Method
posterior_mean(μ::FiniteDistribution) -> Vector

The mean of a distribution over posterior vectors, Σ_π μ(π) π. For posterior_distribution(prior, e) it is the prior, whatever the experiment (the posteriors average back to the prior).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.posterior_payoff — Method
posterior_payoff(problem::DecisionProblem, experiment::Experiment)
    -> (signals::FiniteDistribution, payoff::Matrix)

The decision problem after the experiment, as a table indexed by the signal: the distribution of the signals of positive probability (over their indices) and the posterior expected payoff payoff[a, k] = E[Q[a, θ] | signal k] of each feasible action given each of them. The value of sample information is then the interchange gap of the maximum at the signal distribution,

interchange_gap(ExpectationAlgebra(), signals, payoff) == evsi(problem, experiment),

the same statement that gives EVPI at the prior (interchange_gap).

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.refines — Method
refines(fine::AbstractVector, coarse::AbstractVector) -> Bool

Whether the partition fine refines the partition coarse: two states in the same cell of fine are in the same cell of coarse. A partition is a vector with one cell label per state, as for DecisionAnalysis's evppi. Refinement is a preorder on such vectors (a partial order on the partitions they describe), with the one-cell partition at the bottom (nothing is learned) and the partition into single states at the top (everything is).

julia> refines([:a, :b, :c, :c], [:x, :x, :y, :y])
true

julia> refines([:x, :x, :y, :y], [:a, :b, :c, :c])
false

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.regret_coarsening — Method
regret_coarsening(problem::DecisionProblem, partition::AbstractVector) -> RegretCoarsening

Savage regret as an operation on payoff tables indexed by the menu of actions, and what happens to it when states are merged. With Q[a, θ] the feasible payoffs, p the prior and the partition's cells c, in mass-weighted form:

regret(coarsen(Q))[a, c]  ≤  coarsen(regret(Q))[a, c],

regret is lax under coarsening, and the difference is the same for every action:

gap[c] = Σ_{θ ∈ c} p(θ) max_a Q[a, θ]  −  max_a Σ_{θ ∈ c} p(θ) Q[a, θ]  ≥ 0,

the prior mass of the cell times the expected value of perfect information given the cell (the conditional EVPI). It is zero exactly when one action is best at every state of positive probability in the cell, and the gaps add up to what is still to be learned after learning the cell:

sum(gap) == evpi(problem) − evppi(problem, partition).

The statement is about the prior-weighted regret table and so about expected regret: summing a row over the cells, an action's expected regret in the fine problem exceeds its expected regret in the coarse one by evpi − evppi, the same for every action, so coarsening never reorders actions by expected regret. Nothing is claimed for maximum regret. coarsening_is_lossless decides whether the gap is zero without computing it. This is theorem C6 of the Lean development (expectedRegret_sub_coarseExpectedRegret, coarseRegret_le, coarseExpectedRegret_eq_expectedRegret_iff); no earlier categorical treatment of Savage regret was found by the literature search (stated as "not found", not "does not exist"). The mass-weighted form needs no division, so a cell of probability zero is a column of zeros and not a special case. Needs a single finite prior.

julia> p = DecisionProblem(PayoffTable([0 -10 -60 -150; -20 -20 -25 -90]),
                           [1 // 2, 3 // 10, 3 // 20, 1 // 20]);

julia> r = regret_coarsening(p, [:mild, :mild, :severe, :severe]);

julia> r.gap == [0, 0]     # one action is best throughout each cell
true

julia> sum(regret_coarsening(p, [:all, :all, :all, :all]).gap) == evpi(p)
true

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.value_at_posteriors — Method
value_at_posteriors(problem::DecisionProblem, μ::FiniteDistribution)

The expectation under a distribution over posterior vectors of the best expected payoff at each posterior, Σ_π μ(π) max_a Σ_θ π(θ) Q[a, θ], over the feasible actions. With μ the posterior_distribution of an experiment it is DecisionAnalysis's value_with_information.

CategoricalDecisionAnalysis.value_gap_bound — Method
value_gap_bound(problem::DecisionProblem, e::Experiment, e′::Experiment; kw...)

The deficiency bound on how much more e′ can be worth than e in problem:

information_value(problem, e′) − information_value(problem, e)
    ≤ largest_regret(problem) · deficiency(e, e′).

Value is therefore not only monotone on the garbling preorder but Lipschitz for the deficiency: an experiment that is nearly a garbling of another is worth at most a little more (theorem T6a of the Lean development; after Le Cam). With deficiency zero it is Blackwell's theorem. Computed with DecisionAnalysis's deficiency and deficiency_value_bound; keywords go to deficiency.
