Theorem map

The Lean 4 / Mathlib development in proofs/ (CategoricalDecisionAnalysisProofs) backs the Julia code: a definition there is the finite function a Julia function computes, and a theorem is the property a Julia test asserts, the reason for a refusal or a label, or the soundness of a checker for something Julia exports. The two are separate hand-written texts; no theorem says that the Julia code executes the Lean definitions. What holds them together is this table: the Lean statement, the Julia function it is about, and the test that asserts the same property on random finite inputs.

Where the Julia definitions differ from the Lean ones

  • CVaR. Lean defines cvar as Rockafellar and Uryasev's objective maximised over the atoms; Julia computes the mean of the sorted lower tail. The two were not proved equal in Lean; a test holds them equal on random finite distributions with rational data.
  • The interchange gap. Lean's interchangeGap w W takes W[i, a] (index first, action second); the two-argument Julia method has that layout. The three-argument method takes the payoff table Q[a, θ] of DecisionAnalysis and a value of a monad, and reduces to the Lean form for the distribution monad.
  • The fidelity constant. The Lean chain theorem uses the largest range of one action's payoffs across the states. fidelity_bound reads a general pipeline, where the payoff table is not given but computed, and uses the range of the total utility (utility_range), which is at least as large: the bound is valid and can be looser. The chain certificate takes the range as an argument, and DecisionAnalysis's largest_action_range gives the sharper one.
  • Sequential problems. The Lean theorems are for two stages (and, in nested form, for any horizon with Markov decision rules). The Julia functions take any number of stages and history-dependent policies; beyond two stages the agreement of backward induction with exhaustive search is tested, not proved.
  • The credal monad. Lean has the composition law of the lower pullback for a fixed index type, not the monad. Julia implements the monad on generator lists, with its laws holding up to equality of hulls (same_hull), which is tested.
  • Not formalised: the strategy programme for several decisions and its composition, the fidelity read-off for a general wiring diagram, the structural value-of-information test, and evaluate as the unique functor out of a free Markov category.
